<td style="width: 70%; text-align: justify;">
<p>I am a <em>Full Professor of Robust and Responsible Artificial Intelligence</em> at the Faculty of Computer Science, University of the Bundeswehr Munich (<a href="https://www.unibw.de/" target="_new">UniBwM</a>), and a member of the Research Institute <a href="https://www.unibw.de/code" target="_new">CODE</a> for cyber defence and smart data.</p> 

<p>My research focuses on developing <b>AI systems that remain robust, responsible, and adaptive under real-world conditions</b>. I study how learning systems are affected by distribution shifts, data imbalance, bias, adversarial manipulation, and evolving environments, and develop methods for fairness, robustness, explainability, and reliable adaptation.</p>

<p>A central theme of my work is <b>understanding the capabilities, limitations, and societal implications of AI systems</b>, including modern foundation and generative models. My broader goal is to develop AI systems that remain reliable and responsible throughout their lifecycle and ultimately augment human capabilities and benefit society.</p>

#### Research
My research lies at the intersection of robust AI, responsible AI, adaptive machine learning, and data-centric AI. A unifying question across my work is:<br>
 <strong><em>How can we build AI systems that remain reliable and responsible as their data, environments, users, and societal contexts change?</strong></em>

Research areas:
<ul>
  <li>Robust & Reliable AI (Distribution shifts, Data imbalance, Adversarial robustness, Reliability and evaluation, ...)</li>
  <li>Responsible AI (Bias and Fairness, Explainability, Accountability, AI safety and security, ...)</li>
  <li>Adaptive AI (Data streams, Online learning, ...)</li>
  <li>Generative & Data-centric AI (Foundation models, Data augmentation, Synthetic data, Data quality and bias transfer, ...)</li>
</ul>


#### Teaching
<p>I teach and supervise students in Artificial Intelligence, Machine Learning, Data Mining, Adaptive AI, and Responsible AI.</p>
<p>My approach to teaching and supervision is inspired by Nikos Kazantzakis, who beautifully describes the role of a mentor and educator:</p>
  
<strong><em>"Ideal teachers are those who use themselves as bridges over which they invite their students to cross, then having facilitated their crossing, joyfully collapse, encouraging them to create bridges of their own." </em></strong></p>
